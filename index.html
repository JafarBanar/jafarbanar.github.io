About me

I care about systems that are measured, validated and traceable, whether that is a data pipeline, an LLM answer or a radar detection.

I am a Senior Data Engineer consultant at Sigma Embedded Engineering, most recently on assignment at Volvo Cars, and a dual-degree PhD candidate at Chalmers University of Technology and Eindhoven University of Technology as a Marie Skłodowska-Curie fellow in the EU MyWave network. My thesis is on synchronization and calibration for distributed massive MIMO and coherent distributed radar, with the defence planned for February 2027.

Before that I optimized an on-premises LLM and RAG platform at QRTech, fixed a radar velocity bug in production perception code on an assignment at Einride, and worked on coherent automotive radar during a visiting placement at NXP. Earlier I optimized live 2G/3G/4G networks and did research and simulation studies on 5G and IoT. I also founded AIOpt, a small company building AI for telecom network operations.

Research

My PhD (MyWave ESR 14, supervisors Thomas Eriksson, Alessio Filippi and Alex Alvarado) studies how to keep distributed antennas and radars coherent when each unit has its own free-running oscillator.

  • Closed-form spectral efficiency for distributed massive MIMO under access-point phase noise with MMSE channel estimation.
  • UC-Sync, user-assisted over-the-air phase alignment for user-centric distributed massive MIMO, raising 5th-percentile spectral efficiency by 85% over the unsynchronized baseline.
  • Alamouti-coded distributed zero-forcing that needs no inter-AP phase synchronization.
  • Cramér–Rao bounds and a staged estimator for joint delay, clock-skew and carrier-phase synchronization.
  • Maximum-likelihood clock-drift estimation for bistatic FMCW radar at 78 GHz, started during the NXP placement.
